Maybe they meant it when they said it, but it is not the driving force behind the great SaaS purveyors of our day. Yeah, I am looking at you Microsoft, you too Google, and where do you think you’re hiding Intuit? There is nothing wrong with creating a product that your customer wants to buy, but something is off when the customer is forced to buy something they don’t want. Customer needs are completely secondary to customer lock-in. Sadly, the SaaS model has become too big to care about customers. They spew customer satisfaction surveys at you after every interaction in an effort to show that they care. But these surveys are just another brick in the big data wall. The results are secondary. They kind of care what you think…but mostly they just need you to hang around and keep paying. They collect the data to guide incremental improvements around the edges.也许他们说的时候是认真的，但这并不是当今伟大的 SaaS 供应商背后的驱动力。是的，我在看你们微软，你们也是谷歌，你们认为你们把 Intuit 藏在哪里了？创造客户想要购买的产品并没有什么问题，但当客户被迫购买他们不想要的东西时，就会出现问题。客户需求完全是次要于客户锁定的。可悲的是，SaaS 模式已经变得太大而无法关心客户。他们在每次互动后都会向你进行客户满意度调查，以表明他们的关心。但这些调查只是大数据墙上的另一块砖。结果是次要的。他们有点关心你的想法……但大多数情况下他们只需要你留下来并继续付费。他们收集数据来指导边缘的渐进式改进。

The irony is that just about every SaaS vendor has created the role of customer success manager. These are people that are assigned to your account to help you onboard and have success with the product to keep you from off-boarding. Success doesn’t necessarily translate into helping your organization succeed, just that you ‘succeed’ enough with the product.具有讽刺意味的是，几乎每个 SaaS 供应商都创建了客户成功经理的角色。这些人被分配到您的帐户，以帮助您入职并成功使用该产品，以防止您退出。成功并不一定意味着帮助您的组织取得成功，只是您的产品足够“成功”。

I don’t begrudge anyone’s success in creating a product that customers find useful and want to buy. That is not an easy thing to do. But after a while, the SaaS business model is not about customer success or satisfaction anymore. It is about customer submission and inertia. At a certain point, the customer base becomes too big and the product becomes too big to change. 我不会嫉妒任何人成功地创造出客户觉得有用并想要购买的产品。这并不是一件容易的事。但过了一段时间，SaaS 商业模式就不再关注客户的成功或满意度了。这是关于客户服从和惯性。在某个时刻，客户群变得太大，产品也变得太大而无法改变。

It is the path of least resistance. If everyone else is doing it, it must be good or at least good enough. Plus, there is value in the network effect. Yet, numbers are only safe up to a point. Numbers blind you to unseen risks. Black swan type risks. Rare, but catastrophic. Maybe your backup, disaster recovery, business continuity or whatever they are calling it these days will save you from a single system failing, but it won’t protect you from context and know-how loss. The real problem isn’t loss, it’s accumulation. Too many programs, too many APIs, too many integrations, too much complexity masquerading as sophisticated systems. Context recovery systems don’t exist, yet successful organizations rely on context, not data. Terabytes of data mean nothing without knowing why you have the data, what it means and how you need to make use of it. Maybe the software rules take care of it, but that is a dangerous thing to rely on.也许你的备份、灾难恢复、业务连续性或者他们现在所说的任何东西可以让你免于单个系统故障，但它不能保护你免受上下文和专业知识的损失。真正的问题不是损失，而是积累。太多的程序、太多的 API、太多的集成、太多伪装成复杂系统的复杂性。上下文恢复系统并不存在，但成功的组织依赖于上下文，而不是数据。如果不知道为什么拥有这些数据、这些数据意味着什么以及需要如何使用这些数据，那么数 TB 的数据就毫无意义。也许软件规则可以解决这个问题，但依赖它是一件危险的事情。

Undifferentiated Best Practices无差别的最佳实践无差别的最佳实践

Gotta love the industry ‘best practice’ templates. About a million years ago, I remember an old printed newsletter called the ‘Best Practices Report’ which featured, you guessed it, best practices. The trouble with best practices — then and now — is that they pretend that the world has stopped changing. But the reality is, the world is not static. Things change. You need to keep getting better, you need to keep evolving. Blindly adopting templated best practices is not a path to be best (to paraphrase someone), but rather a path to bland mediocrity.一定喜欢行业“最佳实践”模板。大约一百万年前，我记得一份名为“最佳实践报告”的旧印刷时事通讯，您猜对了，其中介绍了最佳实践。无论是过去还是现在，最佳实践的问题在于它们假装世界已经停止变化。但现实是，世界并不是静止的。事情发生了变化。你需要不断变得更好，你需要不断发展。盲目采用模板化的最佳实践并不是一条通往最好的道路（套用某人的话），而是一条通往平庸的道路。

Let’s Go to the Mall我们去商场吧我们去商场吧

SaaS has become the technology American shopping mall of the 1980s. It is overpriced and predictable. The goods are largely the same in every mall. This is not a dynamic market. It is very much a controlled market. The landlord sets up the platform and the retailers rush in to this great location to make the huge profits and get the advantages of scale. The retailers that can afford the mall’s rates, have very controlled experiences. The mall of the 1980s was not a place of bold experimentation and risk taking. The risk was signing the lease.SaaS 已成为 20 世纪 80 年代美国的技术购物中心。它价格过高且可预测。每个商场的商品基本相同。这不是一个充满活力的市场。这在很大程度上是一个受控的市场。房东搭建平台，零售商蜂拥而入，赚取巨额利润，获得规模优势。能够负担商场价格的零售商拥有非常受控的体验。 20 世纪 80 年代的购物中心并不是一个大胆实验和冒险的地方。风险在于签署租约。
